American Art History: A Featured Object

Watson and the Shark

by John Singleton Copley, 1778

Description

Watson and the Shark is one of the most dramatic and significant paintings in early American art history. It depicts the real-life rescue of Brook Watson who was a young English merchant sailor who was attacked by a shark in Havana Harbor in 1749. Watson lost his leg in the attack but survived. He later commissioned Copley to depict the events in a painting. The painting is historically important because of the prominent inclusion of a Black man at the top of the rescue boat which was an unusually dignified portrayal for the era, and a subject of much art historical discussion.

Why It Matters for American History

Copley was one of colonial America's greatest painters. This work bridges American, British, and Atlantic world history — painted in London by a Boston-born artist, depicting an event in Cuba involving an English merchant. It reflects the interconnected nature of 18th-century Atlantic commerce, race, and power, making it a rich object for exploring American history through art.
